Q1. Is the runtime-isolation root closed?

No.

The heart-path isolation is sound: I found no interleaving where the stalled checker keeps paste blocked after the 100 ms deadline. The timeout can acquire the lock, latch unavailable, and resume paste because the lock is never held across the system call. A serial queue and generation counter are unnecessary.

But the permanent timeout latch is not fully protected:

1. State is `.ready`.
2. A decision enters `availableLanguages`, which stalls.
3. The deadline wins and changes state to `.unavailable(.oracleTimedOut)`.
4. Paste resumes with the original capital.
5. The abandoned call returns and finds the language identifier missing.
6. Its operation body writes `.unavailable(.dictionaryUnavailable)`.
7. `claim()` discards its returned result, but cannot undo that state mutation.

This does not re-block paste or permit another checker call, but it violates the documented permanent-timeout transition and corrupts telemetry. Late operation-side mutations must be conditional on their expected source state.
