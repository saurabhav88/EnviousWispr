Dictating an email starts in the same place as typing one: an empty message and something you want to say. Speak a few sentences, check that the words match your meaning, and make your final edits before sending.

## Start with the message, not the wording

Before recording, decide what the other person needs to know. It might be a short update, a question, or a clear next step. You do not need to compose every sentence in your head first.

For a longer email, jot down three points. Speaking through a small outline makes it easier to keep your message on track.

<aside class="reading-note"><strong>A useful starting point</strong><p>What happened? What does it mean? What needs to happen next?</p></aside>

## Speak a first draft

With your cursor in the message, use your dictation shortcut and speak naturally. Include the greeting and sign-off if you want them in the draft. Pause to think when you need to.

## Review before sending

Read the result once before you send it. Check names, dates, numbers, and anything that could change the meaning of your message. Dictation is a way to get a draft down; you still make the final call on what it says.

- **Names:** Is the recipient's name spelled correctly?
- **Details:** Are dates, amounts, and deadlines right?
- **Tone:** Does it sound like something you would write?

## Make it part of your day

Start with an everyday reply rather than your most important email. Try a short update, a thank-you, or a follow-up after a meeting. You can mix speaking and typing in whatever way feels comfortable.
